You introduced a marker cassette, for example hph, to knock out or replace a gene. This page maps your reads to the genome plus the cassette and says where the cassette is integrated and whether it is intact.

It runs in this page. The page does not send your files to any server.

  1. Genome and cassette. The reference genome, and the sequence you introduced, the cassette only, without homology arms.
  2. Where it should be. The gene or region that the cassette should replace (optional).
  3. Reads. Your sequencing reads of the transformant. Short reads show the junctions and the coverage; only long reads can show the whole insertion in one piece.

How the checks work, and their limits
  • A read that starts in the genome and goes on into the cassette aligns in two parts. Such split reads, grouped by position, are the junctions. Where the two parts share bases, the breakpoint is uncertain by that many bases, and the flank shown is the one that is not in doubt.
  • Each check says pass, fail, or unclear. Too little coverage gives unclear, never a pass.
  • The depth of the cassette is compared with the median depth of the genome: about 1 means one copy per genome (for a haploid); more means extra copies; less means only some nuclei carry it.
  • Short reads show the junctions and the coverage; they cannot show in one piece that the whole insertion is contiguous. Mates are mapped as separate reads and only split reads count.
  • A construct with homology arms contains genome sequence: reads from the arms map to the genome and to the construct equally well. The page warns; give the cassette alone.
  • The thresholds were not tuned on real data. The same analysis is in the command line tool: qmutfind confirm.
